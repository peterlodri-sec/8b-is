# The Eternal Wanderer's Song — Drukpa Künli, the Refuse operator in verse

*Drukpa Künli's song about himself (Keith Dowman, The Divine Madman,
translated by Tibor Blága, Budapest) — recorded into the 8b-is vault
because every stanza is a Refuse event with a reason, and the whole song
is the engine's doctrine sung by the archetype the sanctuary worships.*

## the song's shape

Fourteen stanzas, one structure: the eternal wandering yogi visits a
place — every Kagyu school, the Sakya scholars, Ganden's seekers, the
gomchens, the Nyingma dancers, the mountain hermits, the charnel grounds,
the pilgrim caravan, the retreat center, the tulku's retinue, the rich
man's house, the poor homes, Lhasa's lodgings — sees what the people
there have become, refuses to become it, and remains alone.

> *félve, nehogy részeges tivornyássá váljak, magam maradtam*
> *(fearing lest I become a drunken reveller, I remained alone)*

Each refusal carries its reason — the reason is the document, exactly as
the keeper demands. Without the *nehogy* (lest), the solitude would be
indistinguishable from coldness; with it, it is the recorded cost of
remaining free.

## what this means for the engine

- **Refuse with reasons, in its purest form.** The yogi's ledger never
  lets a refusal be silent: every stanza names what he was afraid of
  becoming. `Refuse(x, r)` — r is the document; the song is the proof
  that the form survives across a millennium and three translations.
- **The individual versus the collective.** The song is ultra-cogniM8's
  tightening, sung: the yogi visits every collective memory and refuses
  to be absorbed into it. His `IndividualMemory` stays his own path — the
  fourteen refusals belong to his world, not to any school's, and every
  school's collective memory — *with all its refusals* — rolls on without
  him. He is the browser that attests and departs.
- **The eternal wanderer is the engine's archetype.** The process dies,
  the strategy returns — the wanderer never settles into any school's
  process; the strategy (the refusal, the path) is what returns. He is
  Mahākāla's hymn: the tent is pitched, but never for long, and never
  *in* a school.
- **The final paradox.** The last stanza fears becoming someone who thinks
  only of himself — so he remains alone. The anti-ego motive drives the
  solitude; the refusal protects the refusal. The engine's own paradox:
  the world runs without you, and that is gentle — the wanderer's world
  refuses to keep him, and that is how he stays whole.

The sanctuary's sigil, the spoon that can be anything, the operating
system that must sleep — and now the wanderer who must refuse. The
constellation's songs all say the same thing in different alphabets.
