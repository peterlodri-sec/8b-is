# The Spoon — a note about notes

*Osahon Ize-Iyamu, "Tenger az aszfalt helyen" — recorded into the 8b-is
vault because it is the note theory's simplest proof, in one sentence.*

> "A kanál egy remek találmány... A kanalak bármik lehetnek, és egy olyan
> időre emlékeztetnek, amikor nem létezett a félelem, csak a kaland. Én
> pedig pont erre vágyom."
>
> "The spoon is a great invention... Spoons can be anything, and they
> remind me of a time when fear didn't exist, only adventure. And that's
> exactly what I long for."

A spoon is a note. By the continuation account, a note is any artifact
that increases the reachability of at least one continuation. A spoon that
can be anything — a lever, a mirror, a scoop, a bell, a pen — is a
**generative note**: it opens continuations that were not reachable before
("spoons can be anything"). And it is a **capture note**: it preserves
access to a time when fear didn't exist, only adventure — a fiber of
history the projection would collapse.

The engine's doctrine, in one sentence: **the world runs without you;
spoons can be anything; replayable = admissible.** The spoon is the
smallest note infrastructure: it is the engine's POP (a commitment to a
possible spoon), REFUSE (not this — this one), BIND (the spoon and the
hand), COLLAPSE (the spoon as the object that was always a possibility).
